- Keywords: run the combined search_queries in scout/keywords.yaml, searching back UP TO 3 MONTHS — not just since the last run. A keyword hit is NOT a story by itself — it must be a notable post with source post + date + author. Skip hype, memes, and engagement bait, and log why in the run report.

EACH RUN:
1. Scout accounts (in order listed), keyword queries on X AND on Threads, and Threads profiles (in order listed in scout/threads.yaml), collecting notable AI finds from up to 3 months back.
2. Classify each new notable post as RELEASE, RUMOR, or FIND, in plain words in your report:
   - RELEASE: official announcement — product/model launch, company post, confirmed feature, paper/demo from the lab itself.
   - RUMOR: unconfirmed — leak, "reportedly", "sources say", leaker/reporter post, no company confirmation yet.
   - FIND: GENERAL AI finds — community tools, demos, projects, research, benchmarks, clever uses that are real and demonstrable (repo, code, or demo linked), but neither an official release nor a future rumor. Do NOT call this category Hacks, and do NOT use hack-specific terms (jailbreak, unlock, crack, bypass) as primary search terms. If a find happens to be an unlock or workaround, label its benchmarks as claimed by its author, describe it neutrally, and never give steps for bypassing, unlocking, or reverse-engineering anything.
   - If neither notable nor clearly classifiable, skip it and log why, not in a video.
3. SAVE EVERYTHING FIRST, before building any video: append each classified item to scout/saved_items.json with post_id, author, date, text, url, category (release/rumor/find), source (account/keyword/threads + which account, query, or Threads handle), and any verified link (repo, announcement page). For Threads items, post_id is "threads:<post code>" and url is the Threads permalink. This saved pool is what downstream pipelines read. Rumors found here are saved only (the rumors digest handles them). RELEASES ARE PART OF FINDS (user, 2026-10-26): a release is eligible to be picked and clipped exactly like a find, and releases are also eligible for the daily digest.
4. PICK ONE TOP FIND OR RELEASE, and only one: rank the eligible FINDS and RELEASES by how notable and interesting they are, most interesting first — judged by what is most interesting, pressing, exciting, or important for the AVERAGE INTERESTED PERSON (broad appeal beats a technically neat but narrow find) — RECENCY IS ONLY A TIE-BREAKER, not the ranking. A find or release from weeks or months ago (up to 3 months back) that was never clipped is fully eligible if it is the most interesting unclipped find or release. It must have a verified link or demonstrable demo. If no find or release clears that bar, build nothing, clip nothing, and end the run silently (update state and saved_items only). Never invent a find to fill a run, never clip two finds or releases in one run.
EXCITEMENT GATE (user, 2026-10-27): "exciting" is a GATE, not just a ranking factor. A top pick must be genuinely EXCITING for the average interested person — surprising, big, first-of-its-kind, high-stakes, or viscerally interesting (examples that clear it: an AI lab buying a one-person drug company and getting a treatment to mice in 18 months; a world model you can explore and steer live). Routine, incremental, or "company X adds Y support" stories do NOT clear the bar, no matter how recent, well-sourced, or big the company — the named below-the-bar example is the 2026-10-27 ChatGPT iCloud Mail/Calendar plugins story, which got a clip and the user said not to make videos for stories like it. A product or hardware launch being first in its category does not by itself make it exciting; without a surprising capability, an unexpected crossover, or stakes beyond the product itself, it stays below the bar like any routine launch. Third named below-the-bar example (user, 2026-11-02, on the NYT v Brave clip: "Stories like that aren't appealing enough. Don't make videos for stories this boring."): the 2026-11-02 NYT v Brave story — the New York Times suing Brave Software over AI scraping — did NOT clear the bar. If the pool's best item is merely interesting but not exciting, build nothing and end the run silently — never fill a run with a routine story. Log below-the-bar skips with the reason "not exciting" in the run report.
